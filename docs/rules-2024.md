# The Fourth Estate: 2024 — Board Game Rules

Oct 7, 2026 · @David Bennett

## Overview

2 to 5 players · ages 12 and up · about 60–90 minutes

Each player runs a news outlet covering the 2024 presidential election. Together you claim the 51 contests of the electoral college for Donald Trump or Kamala Harris, one state at a time. The game ends when either candidate reaches **270 electoral votes**.

You do not score for the states you claim. You score only for the cards you **stake**: cards set aside face down, each a secret bet on a candidate. When a candidate wins, every card staked on that candidate scores its wealth, and the outlet with the most wealth wins.

Every story has two sides, each a real headline: a positive framing on top and an oppositional one below. Use it on its top for its ability, or on its bottom for the other party's currency. Party planks from each 2024 platform stay in play until a rival knocks them out.

The tension is the finish line. Claiming the state that takes a candidate to 270 ends the game, so claim it only when your stakes are ahead. If the finish would not pay you, stall: claim states for the candidate behind.

## Setup

1. Place the map board in the middle of the table. Put a red and a blue vote marker on 0 of the vote track, and the other two on 0 of the House track.
2. Sort the state cards into their three decks by border: large, medium and small. Shuffle each and place it face down on its space. Turn the top card of each face up beside its deck. These first three are not revealed: ignore their reveal headlines.
3. Build the main deck. Shuffle the Harris set (stories and planks marked Harris) face down. Put the switch card on top of it. Shuffle the Biden set and put it on top of the switch. The Biden set is drawn first.
4. Deal 5 cards from the main deck face up into the exchange slots, left to right. The leftmost card is the oldest.
5. Put the Editorials and the 20 Scandals face up on their spaces.
6. Each player takes a player screen, a red and a blue stake pile mat, and a player aid. Deal each player an outlet board at random, or let players choose.
7. Choose a first player at random. Play goes clockwise.
8. Each player shuffles 7 Letters to the Editor and 3 Local Notices as a personal deck. To even out the turn order, the second player adds 1 Editorial from the supply, and the third, fourth and fifth players add 2 each. Then everyone draws 5 cards.

## Currencies and paying

Your hand pays at once. On your turn, every card in your hand counts its currency the moment it is there: at the start of the turn, and whenever you draw. You never play a card just for money. A card counts once a turn, even if it later leaves your hand. Unspent currency is lost at the end of your turn.

| Currency | Icon colour | Spends on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neutral | Grey | Anything |
| Republican | Red | Trump only: a state's Trump half (and its House delegation), or a Republican story or plank |
| Democratic | Blue | Harris only: a state's Harris half (and its House delegation), or a Democratic story or plank |
| Campaign | Gold | States and House delegations only, for either side |

Most cards make neutral plus one party's currency. Pay any price with any mix of currencies that may be spent on it; neutral covers whatever the others do not.

## The states

The 51 contests are the 50 states and D.C., each winner-take-all, with their 2024 electoral votes (538 in all). A state counts toward 270 only after a player claims it.

**Three decks.** The states are split by price into a large deck (11 states, priced 8 or more), a medium deck (18, priced 6–7) and a small deck (22, priced 4–5). Only the top card of each is face up and for sale.

**Prices.** Each half shows its price. The side that won the state in 2024 pays 1 + 1.8 × √EV, rounded: 4 for a 3-vote state, 9 for Pennsylvania, 14 for California. The other side pays 1 more for every full 5 points of the 2024 margin. The seven swing states (Pennsylvania, Georgia, North Carolina, Michigan, Arizona, Wisconsin, Nevada) cost the same either way, as do New Hampshire and Minnesota. Flipping Wyoming costs 13; flipping D.C. costs 20.

**Wealth.** Every state shows its wealth, 12 × EV / 54 rounded and at least 1: California 12, Texas 9, Florida 7, New York 6, down to 1 for the smallest states.

**Powers.** Each half has its own power, and a state card is the strongest card in the game at its price. The swing states' halves also **knock out a plank**: used for Trump, a Democratic plank a rival has in play; for Harris, a Republican one.

**Reveals.** Whenever a state turns face up after a purchase, read its reveal headline aloud: a real article from that state's own press about national coverage or bias. Its effect hits every outlet at once, the buyer included:

- Large deck: every outlet takes a Scandal into its discard pile (Florida, Ohio and Illinois: discards a card at random instead; North Carolina: two).
- Medium deck: every outlet discards a card from its hand at random.
- Small deck: the stories on the exchange go to the bottom of the main deck, and a fresh five are dealt.

**House delegations.** Each state's House delegation is its seats in the House: its electoral votes less 2 (California 52, Texas 38, Wyoming 1; D.C. has none; 435 in all). When you buy a state you may also buy its delegation for the same side, at 1 per 4 seats rounded up (Pennsylvania 17 seats for 5). Only then: a delegation not bought with its state is never for sale again. It matters only in a 269–269 tie.

**Claiming a state.** When you buy a state:

1. Pay the price of the half you claim, plus its delegation if you take it.
2. Put a claim cube of that side's colour on the state's space, and a House disc too if you bought the delegation.
3. Move that side's vote marker up by the state's electoral votes, and its House marker by the seats bought.
4. Turn the card so the claimed half is upright and put it in your discard pile. It is now part of your deck.
5. Turn up the next card of that deck and resolve its reveal.

A claimed state stays claimed for the rest of the game, even if its card is staked. A claimed state card can never be destroyed.

## The main deck

The main deck holds the stories and the party planks, in two sets: the **Biden set** on top (the campaign until July 21, 2024, with Biden as the Democratic figure), then the **switch card**, then the **Harris set**. When the switch card comes up, set it aside and announce the switch: from here on the stories are Harris stories. Each set has the same number of Republican and Democratic planks.

**Stories.** Every story is a real 2024 event, Republican or Democratic. Its currency counts from your hand. When you use it, choose a side:

- **Top** (the positive framing, a headline from the outlet on that party's side): the card's ability, such as draw, destroy, gain, Retraction, a chain, compounding, or +1 of its party.
- **Bottom** (the oppositional framing, a headline from the other side): +1 of the other party's currency (+2 if the story costs 5 or more). Twenty bottoms also knock out a plank of the story's party that a rival has in play.

On the card, the top headline's outlet sits at its lower right and the bottom headline's outlet at its upper right.

**Party planks.** Twenty planks carry the wording of the 2024 Republican and Democratic platforms. Use a plank to put it into play in front of you. It stays there, giving you its bonus at the start of each of your turns, while every other outlet gets +1 of the plank's party currency at the start of theirs, until a rival knocks it out. A plank knocked out goes to its owner's discard pile.

| Plank cost | Each turn | Wealth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | +1 neutral | 3 |
| 5 | Draw 1 card | 3 |
| 6 | +2 neutral | 3 |
| 7 | +1 neutral and draw 1 card | 3 |

## Playing a turn

At the start of your turn, take the bonus of each plank you have in play, then choose one: **stake** or **play**.

### Stake

Instead of your whole turn:

1. Choose one card from your hand and decide which candidate to stake it on.
2. Place it face down on your red (Trump) or blue (Harris) stake pile mat, behind your screen.
3. Announce "I stake" and the number of cards you now have staked. Never reveal the card or the side.
4. Discard the rest of your hand and draw 5.

A staked card leaves your deck for good.

### Play

1. **Use cards.** Use any card in your hand that does something: a story on its top or its bottom, a plank into play, a state's power. Put it in your play area and resolve it. Cards with nothing to use, such as Letters, Local Notices and Editorials, stay in hand and simply pay. Cards you draw count their currency at once.
2. **Buy states (any number).** Buy any face-up state for the side you choose, with its House delegation if you like. Each purchase turns up the next state of that deck, and its reveal hits every outlet.
3. **Buy cards (any number).** Buy any story or plank on the exchange you can pay for, or an Editorial from its pile. Bought cards go to your discard pile; do not refill the exchange yet.
4. **Clean up.** Planks you used stay in front of you. Discard every other card you used and every card left in your hand. Draw 5 cards.

You may buy states and cards in any order. When your deck is empty and you need to draw, shuffle your discard pile to form a new deck.

### End of every turn

1. Refill the exchange to 5 cards from the main deck. If the switch card comes up, set it aside and deal the next card.
2. **The news cycle.** Move the oldest card in the exchange (the leftmost) to the bottom of the main deck. Slide the others left and deal a new card into the rightmost slot.

The next player clockwise takes a turn.

| Top ▲ | Use the story's ability (the positive framing) |
| Bottom ▼ +N | Take N of the other party's currency instead (the oppositional framing) |
| Draw N | Draw N cards from your deck into your hand; you may use them this turn |
| Destroy | You may remove a card in your hand or discard pile from the game. Claimed state cards cannot be destroyed |
| Gain a story costing N or less | Take a story or plank from the exchange into your discard pile for free; refill that slot at once |
| Compounding | +1 neutral for each other story of the same kind you use this turn (top side only) |
| Chain | A bonus if you use another story of the same kind this turn (top side only) |
| Per 3 states | +1 of the card's party currency for every 3 state cards you own |
| Retraction | Draw a card, then you may destroy a Scandal in your hand or discard pile |
| Knock out a plank | Choose a plank of the named party that a rival has in play; it goes to its owner's discard pile |
| Plank | Stays in play after you use it: its bonus at the start of each of your turns, +1 of its party to every rival at the start of theirs |
| Reveal | When a state turns face up after a purchase, its effect hits every outlet: a Scandal, a discard at random, or a fresh exchange |
| House | Buy a state's delegation with the state, for the same side; it decides a 269–269 tie |
| Defense | No effect in this edition (see Designer's notes) |

When the Scandal pile is empty, a reveal's Scandals are not taken.

## Ending the game and scoring

**270.** The moment either vote marker reaches 270, that candidate has won the election. The player who claimed that state finishes their turn.

**The last round.** Play continues clockwise until every player has had the same number of turns, so the round ends with the player to the right of the first player. These last turns are played normally, and a stake now is a sure bet.

**The House.** If every state has been claimed and neither candidate has 270 (a 269–269 tie), the game ends at the end of that turn and the House decides: the candidate with more House seats bought wins. If the House is tied too, the election is deadlocked and no stake scores.

**Scoring.** Every player lifts their screen and reveals both stake piles.

- Each card on the winner's pile scores its wealth. A staked Letter to the Editor or Local Notice costs 1.
- Cards on the loser's pile score nothing.
- Cards in your deck, hand, discard pile and play area score nothing.

The player with the most wealth wins. On a tie, the tied player who claimed the most electoral votes wins.

**Trigger or stall.** Because the deciding claim ends the game, cross 270 only when your stakes are ahead. If they are not, claim states for the candidate behind to keep the leader short of 270 while you stake. A staked state still counts for the side it was claimed for, whichever pile it sits on.
